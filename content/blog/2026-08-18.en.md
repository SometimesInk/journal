## Section I -- Introduction to Conlanging

It was during my penultimate year in highschool that I started to *conlang*, to construct languages, although it was more similar to copying whatever information I would find from guides on the internet, misread wikipedia pages and blog articles on language construction into my Google Sheets and calling it a language more than the actual practice of researching linguistics to use as a basis for a fictional language. Conlanging is an art form long forgotten (perhaps never remembered), but an art form nevertheless deserving of respect, and esteem, I hide this is the least you will remember from this article.

When I first made my language, Asiuka, my only point of reference was my prior surface-level study of Ancient Greek morphology which made my goal of attaining the very specific phonoæsthetics[^1] I wanted very difficult (after all, I had never studied phonetics or phonology). See this example sentence from the [Conlang Syntax Test Cases](https://cofl.github.io/conlang/resources/mirror/conlang-syntax-test-cases.html) website translated to this horrid language:

<div data-gloss>
  <p>εi̯ʰty kylufa=la mi aʰ=tεi̯kɯpai̯ eʰ=k&lt;iy̯&gt;pa</p>
  <p>we-PL wait=PST  for FOR=event NE=&lt;ADV&gt;success</p>
  <p>"We waited for the event unsuccessfully"</p>
</div>

*Yes, verb declension is done through postparticles: I took the phrase* protolang *literally... As a second note, I am using phonetic transcription because past self's romanization is unusable (foreshadowing).*

One of the first things that would jump to the mind of a more experienced conlanger straightaway is the usage of /h/ in postvocalic positions, although is much more common in prevocalic positions[^2] (Davis & Cho 2003). Another one would be the vowel system containing /a/ and /ε/ yet no low or mid back vowel: not very symetric, which vowel inventories usually are, to a degree. It has /ɯ/ which is pretty a rare phoneme too. To resume, my first language was poorly created; the following flaws may be defined.

1. Unnaturalistic phonology and morphology
2. Poorly defined syntax
3. No prosody
4. No romanization

The biggest to my past self was the lack of naturalism which I sought to work on in my next conlang.

Around the time my first conlang reached a state of completion (although this concept barely exists), I started the long journey of language learning. Japanese, which I had chosen as an inspiration for my Asiuka, was the language which interested me the most, hence I started studying away in my free time. My study of languages (not of linguistics) will not be refered hereafter. Now having a new perspective on language, I worked for the next year on a family of language named Serain, the topic of this article.
